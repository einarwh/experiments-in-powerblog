# The imitation of value

<p class="blog-post-date">May 8, 2026</p>

Much ado has been made over the imitation game. A much more prevalent and pressing issue is another kind of imitation game, where what’s been imitated is not a human being, but value. Value is on everybody’s mind, but is a slippery thing to define. Or maybe it’s very easy: value is shorthand for something is considered by valuable by someone in some context or for some purpose. If that someone is a consumer or a customer, they may be willing to pay for it. If that someone is a professional doing some sort of work, it means that it helps them in their work. (Not at all what I want to say.) 

Perceived value. Something that looks like a valuable artefact. It could be a report, it could be a plan, or it could be the ability to create summaries, minutes, transcripts, or a synthetic podcast. Syntactic level. Impressive looks. Depth and thoroughness. “A useful starting point”.

What is its value? Is it valuable?

Where does the artifact touch ground, make contact with reality? With the producer or the consumer? 

A bad starting point is much worse than no starting point at all. It represents a framing of the task or the problem, an anchoring and a tethering that must be broken free from. It is a false start. It is made all the much worse due to the social factors in rejecting someone's artifact, in fact declaring it worthless. In that way, the value of the artifact is definitely negative.
